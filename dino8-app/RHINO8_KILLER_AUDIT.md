# Dino 8 vs. Rhino 8: Full Self-Audit ("Does Dino 8 beat Rhino 8 at literally everything?")

Date: 2026-09-08
Scope: every axis a user would judge a NURBS/mesh/SubD modeler on, not just command-name coverage.
Method: for each category, I inspected the actual Dino 8 source/build/CI (cited by file) and compared it
against Rhino 8's real, shipping behavior (McNeel's commercial release, version 8, as of this audit).
This is a self-critical audit, not a marketing sheet: **honest "loses" are called out explicitly, and
every one gets a concrete task in the backlog at the end.**

## Verdict up front

**No. Dino 8 does not beat Rhino 8 at everything today.** It matches or exceeds Rhino 8 on cost,
command-name breadth, and a handful of specific things (see wins below). It loses — sometimes badly —
on kernel robustness, performance/scale, rendering quality, ecosystem, platform reach, polish, trust,
and support. Command coverage (1031/24/0 of 1055) measures "does a command exist and produce a
plausible result on a simple test case," not "does it hold up on the hostile, messy, huge, decades-old
files real users throw at Rhino." Those are different bars, and Dino 8 has only cleared the first one.

## Where Dino 8 genuinely wins today

1. **Price and licensing model.** Zero cost, zero accounts, zero telemetry, verified by direct source grep (no "subscription", "license key", "payment", or "telemetry" code paths — only free-software statements). This is real and durable.
2. **Command-name coverage.** 1031/1055 + 131 bonus commands is a larger nominal command surface than most Rhino competitors ever attempt, and it was earned honestly (verified via the real `Application::RegisterCommands()` order, not an inflated naive scan).
3. **A handful of specific technical claims that are honestly true and cite-able:** the fixed `ShrinkWrap` SDF/marching-cubes implementation, the real B-rep edge fillets matching analytic volumes on tested cases, a real (if CPU-only) path tracer, real SpaceMouse support, real sketch constraints. These are not stubs — they do real work, which is more than most "modeler in a weekend" clones manage.
4. **Undo reliability claim.** The full-snapshot model genuinely cannot exhibit the "undo silently stops working" class of bug that Rhino users do occasionally report — this is a legitimate, if narrow, correctness win, at a real and documented performance cost.

## Where Dino 8 loses, and why it matters

The most important losses, in order of how much they'd matter to a real user deciding between the two products:

1. **Kernel/algorithm robustness on hard, real-world geometry.** This is the single biggest gap. Command coverage says "FilletEdge exists and works on a 10x10x10 box." Rhino's 30-year edge in this exact area is surviving the fillet/boolean/intersection failures that come from real, messy, imported geometry (bad tangencies, near-degenerate surfaces, huge tolerances). Dino 8 has no adversarial test corpus and no evidence it survives these cases.
2. **Performance at scale.** No profiling, no large-assembly stress test, a full-document-snapshot undo, and a mostly single-threaded core. A 50,000-object Rhino file that pans and orbits smoothly in Rhino 8 has never been tested in Dino 8 and likely will not perform comparably.
3. **Rendering quality.** CPU-only path tracing vs. Rhino's GPU-accelerated Cycles integration plus its entire third-party render-plugin ecosystem (V-Ray, KeyShot, Enscape, etc.) is not a close contest.
4. **Ecosystem network effects.** Grasshopper's plugin ecosystem, Python/RhinoCommon scripting maturity, and thousands of existing plugins/definitions cannot be matched by engineering effort alone — this requires either years of organic third-party adoption or a deliberate compatibility strategy (see backlog item 5 below).
5. **Trust and distribution.** Unsigned installers are a first-impression killer: Windows SmartScreen and macOS Gatekeeper will actively warn users away before they ever see the free command coverage.
6. **QA maturity.** ~949 scripted smoke checks is meaningful regression protection for *this* team's own changes, but it is not remotely comparable to Rhino's real-world QA surface (millions of user-files, decades of bug reports).
7. **No touch/tablet or cloud story.** Rhino for iPad and Rhino Compute/ShapeDiver represent entire product lines Dino 8 has no answer to.
8. **Associativity.** GD&T/tables/annotations/Dino Flow bakes are all static/baked, not live-linked — a meaningful workflow regression for any iterative design process.

## Backlog: what it would take to actually "destroy Rhino 8"

Grouped by priority (P0 = existential gaps that block "better than Rhino at everything"; P1 = large but tractable; P2 = polish/ecosystem plays that take time regardless of engineering effort).

### P0 — kernel and performance (the credibility gaps)
- [x] Adversarial geometry test corpus: `tests/boolean_adversarial_script.txt` and `tests/fillet_adversarial_script.txt` cover near-tangent/barely-overlapping/coincident/sliver/huge-scale/10-deep-chained/non-manifold booleans and tiny/at-limit/too-large-radius/huge-scale/shallow-bend fillets. Root-cause fixes landed: boolean mesh/Manifold tolerances now scale to object size instead of fixed epsilons (`dino8-kernel/src/boolean.cpp`, `src/commands/cmd_common.h`), and `FilletEdge` now verifies real tessellated closure before calling a result "exact" instead of trusting topology alone (`src/commands/cmd_fillet.cpp`). A second pass added `tests/curve_adversarial_script.txt`: `PlanarSrf` and a solid-capping `Extrude` both used to silently build a geometrically corrupt result (a Brep whose single trim loop crosses itself in 2D) from a self-crossing closed curve; a shared `CurveSelfIntersects` helper (`src/commands/cmd_common.h`) now rejects that boundary with a clear diagnostic instead. Honestly not reached in that second pass: high-aspect-ratio surfaces and self-intersecting sweep/loft paths, noted as open (not fixed, not proven infeasible) in `tests/adversarial_corpus_notes.md`. One genuine kernel-level limit remains and is documented, not hidden, in the same file: `dino8::kernel::Mesh`'s single-precision (`ON_3fPoint`) vertex storage can't represent small features at ~1e6-unit coordinate scales — this would need a kernel-wide double-precision mesh migration, out of scope for this pass. This corpus is still hundreds of cases smaller than "hundreds of real-world hard .3dm/STEP files"; it's real hardening, not parity with 30 years of user-file exposure (see Bottom line). A later pass extended the closed-form exact variable-radius `FilletEdge`/`FilletSrf` (previously exact only for two planar faces) to the plane+perpendicular-cylinder case — a flat face meeting a cylindrical bore or boss, the second most common real-world fillet shape — verified against the real binary with no fallback-to-approximate suffix in the printed result (`BuildPlaneCylinderVariableFillet`, `src/commands/cmd_fillet.cpp`; regression case in `tests/fillet_script.txt`). While verifying it, found and partly fixed a genuine, previously-undetected, pre-existing bug: `FilletEdge`'s mesh fallback (used whenever the exact B-rep trim path can't apply, e.g. any edge with a non-planar adjacent face) never actually closes correctly on a *closed* edge — a solid cylinder's own flat-top rim, or a bore's rim, both full 360-degree loops — because it always treated the sampled spine as an open chain. Fixed the loft/spine-closure half of that (`Mesh::LoftPeriodicRings()` in `dino8-kernel`, verified against `Mesh::Torus()`'s own reference volume; `BuildFillet`'s own last-row-vs-first-row snap). A second, deeper bug in the same fallback was found in the process — welding an independently-tessellated open fillet ribbon onto a mesh that a boolean difference of two closed solids always makes fully closed by construction is topologically impossible at any tolerance — and is now fully fixed too, via a real architecture change: the fillet is built as its own closed solid (`BuildFilletSolid`) and combined with a boolean UNION instead of a vertex weld, letting Manifold's own CSG engine compute the true intersection between two independently-tessellated closed solids. A proportional volume-magnitude sanity check (removed-volume must be within 10% of the cutter's own volume) guards against a further single-precision-quantization failure mode found while verifying this at large coordinate scales. Verified end to end on the real cylinder-rim case with a permanent regression test (`tests/fillet_script.txt`); full diagnosis and the fix's rationale in `tests/adversarial_corpus_notes.md` §3.
- [x] Malformed/crafted-file hardening: a later pass applied one specific lens across every trust boundary in the codebase - does an externally-supplied value (a file's data, a plugin's data) ever reach an array index, a recursion depth, or an allocation size without validation? Found and fixed 8 real bugs this way, several confirmed as genuine crashes (not just theoretical) via a minimal standalone reproduction of the exact pattern before and after the fix: (1) the plugin ABI's `add_mesh`/`make_mesh_value` (`src/plugins/PluginManager.cpp`) copied a plugin-supplied `faces[]` array's vertex indices into the mesh with no bounds check; (2) `FileIgesStep.cpp`'s `BuildIgesCurve` recursed into a type-102 composite curve's own children with no depth/cycle guard - a self-referencing composite curve in a crafted IGES file drove unbounded recursion, **confirmed as a real stack-overflow segfault** by temporarily disabling the fix and reproducing it on a hand-crafted fixture (`tests/iges_recursive_fixture.igs`); (3) the shared JSON parser (`src/util/json_mini.cpp`, the single entry point for every settings/i18n/`.dflow`/plugin-manifest file) had the same unbounded-nesting-recursion bug, **confirmed via a standalone stripped-down reproduction segfaulting on a 200,000-deep nested array**; (4) a Dino Flow Gene Pool node's `gene_count`, read straight from a `.dflow` file's JSON with only a floor, could force a multi-GB allocation or hit undefined behavior in the `double`→`int` cast for an out-of-range value (`src/flow/FlowGraph.cpp`); (5) sketch constraint solving (`src/sketch/Constraints.cpp`) validated a constraint's `points`/`radius_objects` array lengths against the wrong helper function (one that counts distinct geometry objects, not the raw point/radius-object slots the solver actually indexes), so a constraint persisted in a document's own user-text with too few points reached an unconditional out-of-bounds vector read - **confirmed as a real crash via a minimal ASan repro** of the exact indexing pattern; (6) a Table annotation's `rows`/`cols` (`src/drafting/Table.cpp`) and (7) an Arch stair's `step_count` (`src/arch/ArchComponents.cpp`), both persisted in object/document user-text, had no upper bound before driving a `resize`/loop; (8) a Cage's `CageDivisions` tag (`src/commands/cmd_solidtools.cpp`) had an integer-overflow gap in its own sanity check that could let a forged, wildly out-of-range value pass validation and reach an out-of-bounds lattice index. Every fix has a permanent regression test. Two follow-up sweeps of the same lens over GPU/render code, the native `.3dm` loader, OBJ/PLY/STL import, and Dino Flow's own node/port/wire indices came back clean - already disciplined.
- [x] Performance: `--stress N` + `tests/stress.sh`, a uniform spatial grid for viewport picking (`src/spatial/ObjectGrid.*`), and a header-only thread pool for parallelizable batch work (`src/util/ThreadPool.h`), with real measured numbers in `tests/performance_notes.md` — picking sped up 6.8x at 1,000 objects up to 248x at 50,000 objects. A later pass closed the renderer's own gap: `Viewport::DrawObjects` now culls objects entirely outside the current view frustum (a conservative AABB-vs-6-plane test, correct for both perspective and ortho, objects showing control points always exempted) before building draw calls, using the same spatial grid as a broad phase. Verified the way a correctness-critical cull has to be: `tests/cull_test.sh` proves cull-on and cull-off renders of the same scene are byte-for-byte pixel-identical, not just that draw-call counts dropped. A real hang bug (the grid's cell-size heuristic starving on a flat/2D scene's near-zero volume) was found and fixed in the process. Honestly still unfixed: `Document::BeginChange`'s per-command undo cost on the ~370 sites not yet on the fast path, and `PickPoint`/control-point picking (deliberately not grid-indexed — control points can lie outside their curve's tessellated bbox); no level-of-detail (LOD) simplification was attempted, only visibility culling.
- [x] Diff-based undo/redo replacing full-document snapshots (`src/doc/Document.h/.cpp`): a `StateDelta`/`HistoryEntry` model stores only added/removed/modified objects instead of a full deep copy every command, with periodic full checkpoints as a correctness anchor. Measured in `tests/undo_scaling_notes.md`: ~550x cheaper to record an edit and 14-43x faster undo/redo on a 12,000-object document via the fast path, wired first into `ApplyXform`'s in-place transforms and later extended to 12 more single/fixed-selection commands (`Hide`, `SetObjectName`, `SetUserText`, `Lock`, `ChangeLayer`'s existing-layer case, `ChangeToCurrentLayer`, `MatchLayer`, `SetRenderColor`, `MatchProperties`, `SoftEdit`, `MoveUVN`, `DrapePt`). Honestly scoped: the other ~370 `BeginChange` sites (whole-document scans like `Unlock`/`UnlockSelected`, or ones that add/remove objects or mutate document-level state like layers) still use the safe general path — extending further is future work, not done here.
- [x] Multi-threading beyond the path tracer: a real thread pool (`src/util/ThreadPool.h`) now parallelizes display-mesh warmup and PLY export tessellation. `ArrayHole`'s cutter construction was deliberately kept serial — it calls into Manifold/OpenNURBS construction whose thread-safety under concurrent calls isn't established anywhere in this codebase, and risking silent corruption wasn't worth the speedup. Command engine and Document mutation remain single-threaded by design.
- [x] GPU-accelerated real-time rendering: the previously-fake `RayTraced` display mode (it was blitting the CPU path tracer's output as a static texture) now runs a genuine per-frame fragment-shader BVH traversal (`src/render/GpuRaytracer.*`) — direct lighting, 1-3 configurable indirect bounces with roughness-based early termination (was a hardcoded single bounce), material texture sampling via a small 16-layer/64x64 atlas, straight-through alpha-test transparency, temporal accumulation, and an edge-aware bilateral denoiser, on GL 3.3 core (no compute shaders, since macOS caps at GL 4.1). **A second, deeper audit pass found this had actually been completely non-functional in this session's own test environment since the texture/transparency/multi-bounce commit landed**: the trace shader used `mat2` (GLSL's reserved 2x2-matrix type name) as a plain variable name, a genuine shader-compile-breaking bug that `GpuRaytracer::Init()` was silently swallowing - `tests/smoke.sh`'s only assertion (`gl_error=0`) passed vacuously because a fully-broken raytracer that never runs also never errors. Fixed by renaming the variable and adding a real assertion (`rt_accum_frames=N>0 empty=0`, via a new `DINO8_RT_FRAMES` env hook) that a frame was actually produced - exactly the gap `gpu_render_notes.md` had itself flagged as unclosed. Honest gap, still documented in `tests/gpu_render_notes.md`: no hardware RT cores, area lights still approximated as point lights, transparency is straight-through not real refraction (no Fresnel, shadow rays still treat every surface as opaque), the texture atlas is a fixed low-resolution tile (not full-resolution per-material textures), and a fixed (not ML) denoiser — a real step toward Rhino's Raytraced mode, not parity with it.
- [ ] (in progress, multi-session effort) **A genuine, from-scratch exact B-rep kernel layer, built only from published/public-domain computational-geometry algorithms** — explicitly not a copy or reverse-engineering of Parasolid's own (proprietary, patent-covered) implementation, and not yet a claim of matching it. The existing `BooleanCombine` intersects two solids' *tessellated meshes* via the Manifold library, so its precision is capped by mesh chord tolerance; the new code (`dino8-kernel/include/dino8/kernel/boolean.h`, `src/boolean.cpp`, `src/brep.cpp`) operates directly on `Brep`'s own planar-face polygons (`Brep::PlanarFaces()`/`FromPlanarFaces()`, plane computed via Newell's method so polygon and plane always agree) at full double precision: `BooleanIntersectConvexPlanar` (Sutherland–Hodgman 1974 half-space clipping) for two convex solids; `BooleanCombinePlanar` (Requicha & Voelcker, *Boundary Representation and Solid Modeling*, IEEE 1985's split/classify/combine boundary-evaluation scheme — golden-ratio-direction ray casting for point-in-solid classification, with graze/parallel retry) for general **non-convex** Union/Intersection/Difference; `ShellConvexPlanar` for exact convex hollow-shelling with per-opening rim closure; `FilletConvexEdge` (`dino8-kernel/include/dino8/kernel/fillet.h`, `src/fillet.cpp`) for an exact rolling-ball fillet on a straight convex edge shared by two planar faces — the classical closed-form result (Rossignac & Requicha, "Constant-radius blending in solid modeling," 1984) that a ball of fixed radius rolled along a straight dihedral edge traces exactly a circular-cylinder patch, realized here as the kernel's first `Brep::CylindricalFace` (an exact rational-NURBS cylinder patch via `ON_Cylinder::GetNurbForm`, trimmed to the true swept angle — `Brep::FromMixedFaces` now assembles solids from a mix of planar and cylindrical faces). A genuine sub-problem was found and fixed during its own verification, not glossed over: when the filleted edge runs the full width between two other faces perpendicular to it, those end faces still have their original sharp corner unless it's replaced by the same arc — left unfixed, this measurably changes the closed solid's volume (confirmed by direct debugging), so `FilletConvexEdge` also notches any such perpendicular end face with a fine polygonal approximation of the exact arc (the one place this function's geometry isn't exact to floating-point precision, by construction of `PlanarFace`'s straight-edged-loop representation, not by approximation of the fillet math itself). An oblique (non-perpendicular) end face is deliberately left untouched, a disclosed narrower scope than the general "vertex blend" problem. Verified with a real closed-form-vs-tessellated-volume check (`TestFilletConvexEdgeUnitCubeTopFrontCorner`, `r²(1-π/4)` against measured volume) plus rail-curve/re-trim boundary agreement checks at 1e-9 and two rejection cases. All of `BooleanIntersectConvexPlanar`/`BooleanCombinePlanar`/`ShellConvexPlanar`/`FilletConvexEdge` are verified to ~1e-9 with a double-precision volume helper (`PlanarBrepVolumeExact`), not just the ~1e-6 single-precision mesh floor the rest of the kernel is limited to — an L-shaped non-convex prism vs. a box (`TestBooleanCombinePlanarNonConvexLShapeVsBox`) checks Union/Intersection/A-minus-B/B-minus-A against hand-computed exact volumes and the partition identity, cross-checked against Manifold's own mesh-based watertightness.
