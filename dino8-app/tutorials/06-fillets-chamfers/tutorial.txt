#title: Fillets and chamfers
#summary: Round one edge with FilletEdge and bevel another with ChamferEdge, and check the volume change numerically.
#level: Intermediate
# Source: extends tests/fillet_script.txt (FilletEdge / ChamferEdge sections) and docs/tutorial_scripts/10_profile_extrude.txt.
#narrate: Sharp edges rarely survive in real parts. Fillets round an edge and chamfers bevel it. We will use both, on two identical blocks.
SetActiveViewport Perspective
MaxViewport
Box 0,0,0 10,10,0 10
Box 20,0,0 30,10,0 10
ZoomExtents
@expect_objects 2
#narrate: Two ten-unit cubes side by side. Each has a volume of one thousand cubic units.
SelNone
SelID 1
Volume
#narrate: The first cube will get a fillet. Volume confirms we start at exactly one thousand.
SelNone
FilletEdge
Radius=2
10,10,5
Enter
@expect_objects 2
#narrate: FilletEdge asks for a radius and then for an edge to round. We set Radius to two, then point at the middle of one vertical edge. Enter accepts.
SelNone
SelID 1
Volume
#narrate: A rolling ball of radius two rounded the edge, so the volume dropped to nine hundred ninety-one point four cubic units.
SelNone
ChamferEdge
Radius=2
30,10,5
Enter
@expect_objects 2
#narrate: ChamferEdge works the same way, but cuts a flat bevel instead of a curve. Distance two on each face.
SelNone
SelID 2
Volume
#narrate: A bevel of distance two on a ten unit edge removes two times two over two, times ten, which is exactly twenty cubic units. Nine hundred eighty remain.
ZoomExtents
#narrate: Use fillets where a smooth, flowing surface or stress relief matters, and chamfers where you want a cheap, crisp, machinable bevel.
