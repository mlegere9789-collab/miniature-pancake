# Sweep1 with SEVERAL cross-sections -> the kernel's exact Brep::Sweep1Sections
# (Sweep1Command::Build, cmd_surface.cpp). Every case builds its rail and
# sections on its own layer, runs Sweep1 (rail by name, sections by layer),
# measures the result with the app's own commands (Area / BoundingBox /
# ClosestPt on the finished surface) and clears the
# document. smoke.sh cuts the output
# apart per case at each "Command: Sweep1" line.

# --- Case A: two circles on a straight rail = a cone frustum.
# rail (0,0,0)-(0,0,10); circle r = 2 at z = 0 and r = 1 at z = 10.
# Closed form: lateral area pi (r0 + r1) sqrt(h^2 + (r0 - r1)^2) = 94.72 (the
# solid's volume 73.304 is checked in the kernel test; Cap does not take a Surface).
NewLayer S1ARail
Line 0,0,0 0,0,10
SelLast
SetObjectName S1ARail
NewLayer S1ASections
Circle 0,0,0 2
Circle 0,0,10 1
SelNone
SelName S1ARail
Sweep1
SelLayer
Enter
@expect_objects 4
SelSrf
Area
BoundingBox
# points ON the two section circles (seam point, quarter, half): the surface
# must pass through them
SelNone
SelSrf
ClosestPt
2,0,0
Enter
SelNone
SelSrf
ClosestPt
0,2,0
Enter
SelNone
SelSrf
ClosestPt
-1,0,10
Enter
SelNone
SelSrf
ClosestPt
0,1,10
Enter
# a point on the cone half way up (r = 1.5 at z = 5): on the surface too
SelNone
SelSrf
ClosestPt
1.5,0,5
Enter
SelAll
Delete
@expect_objects 0

# --- Case B: three DIFFERENT sections on a straight rail: circle r = 2 at
# z = 0, a 6 x 3 rectangle (closed polyline starting on its +x edge, so its
# seam lines up with the circles') at z = 5 and a circle r = 1 at z = 10.
NewLayer S1BRail
Line 0,0,0 0,0,10
SelLast
SetObjectName S1BRail
NewLayer S1BSections
Circle 0,0,0 2
Polyline 3,0,5 3,1.5,5 -3,1.5,5 -3,-1.5,5 3,-1.5,5 3,0,5
Enter
Circle 0,0,10 1
SelNone
SelName S1BRail
Sweep1
SelLayer
Enter
SelSrf
BoundingBox
# on the rectangle: the middle of its +y edge, a point of its +x edge, a corner
SelNone
SelSrf
ClosestPt
0,1.5,5
Enter
SelNone
SelSrf
ClosestPt
3,-1,5
Enter
SelNone
SelSrf
ClosestPt
-3,1.5,5
Enter
# on the two circles
SelNone
SelSrf
ClosestPt
0,-2,0
Enter
SelNone
SelSrf
ClosestPt
-1,0,10
Enter
SelAll
Delete
@expect_objects 0

# --- Case C: a CURVED rail (cubic through (0,0,0) (0,0,4) (3,0,6) (3,0,10);
# its z = 5 point is exactly (1.5,0,5)) with circles r = 1.5, 2, 1 centred on
# it at z = 0, 5, 10.
NewLayer S1CRail
Curve 0,0,0 0,0,4 3,0,6 3,0,10 Enter
SelLast

NewLayer S1CSections
Circle 0,0,0 1.5

Circle 3,0,10 1
SelNone

SelLayer
Enter
SelSrf
BoundingBox
SelNone
SelSrf
ClosestPt
3.5,0,5
Enter
SelNone
SelSrf
ClosestPt
1.5,-2,5
Enter
SelNone
SelSrf
ClosestPt
1.5,0,0
Enter
SelNone
SelSrf

4,0,10
Enter
SelAll

# --- Case D (refusal -> fallback): the second circle sits in the plane
# z = 20, which the rail (z = 0..10) never reaches. The kernel refuses with
# that reason, the command prints it and still builds the old sampled sweep.
NewLayer S1DRail
Line 0,0,0 0,0,10
SelLast
SetObjectName S1DRail
NewLayer S1DSections
Circle 0,0,0 2
Circle 0,0,20 1
SelNone
SelName S1DRail
Sweep1
SelLayer
Enter
@expect_objects 4
SelAll
Delete
@expect_objects 0
